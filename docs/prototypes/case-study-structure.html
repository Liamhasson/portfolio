At a glance 0.3

Research, design & build, end to end · Mar 2026 – ongoing · Solo · Figma, Figma MCP, Claude Code, Next.js, Vercel · Ticketmaster + PredictHQ APIs

Two bands, one lost night 1

The origin story (the release show lost to an arena show; a friend's lost to the Champions League final), then the problem. Personas, market and competitor table compressed onto this one screen.

Danger Score, live 2

Signature interaction: capacity × genre × timing, run live on the Berlin example. The formula is visible as you play with it.

One search, two APIs 1

Ticketmaster for ticketed events + PredictHQ for everything else. Followed dates and updates.

Under the hood: same-name cities ("Cambridge": 124 events across UK + US → 21) · tour shows wanting the same night → two-pass assignment

CHALLENGES
What got in the way 1
  • A blank calendar looked "safe": no data read as no competition (London showed 7 days, Tel Aviv a green "No conflicts") → "Not checked" states, holidays shown unscored, an "announced" indicator from an 8-city count. London: 7 → 31 days
  • The 0–100 score claimed precision it didn't have ("Safe 18" read as a date) → Safe · Threat · Critical only, cut-points checked on 3,172 real listings
  • A feature cut the same day: distance to competing shows asked for the venue first, the very answer people come to find → removed
Outcome 0.5

Live at eventread.vercel.app, built solo. Finds every competing event, ticketed or not, before the date is booked. Try it →

No user numbers: not launched to market yet. That story belongs in the interview.

Cyvore · B2B website. A cybersecurity startup needed a site investors would believe in. The four-panel hero.

A linear site vs an investor meeting 1

Why a scroll-through website fails when a founder is presenting live. You pitched the fix unasked.

One screen, everything 2

Signature interaction: a working replica of the 4-tab hero (Security · Exposure · Coverage · Enables).

Before / after 1

Drag slider: navigation and micro-animation, old site vs new. The design system shrinks to one strip (type, tokens, states).

CHALLENGES
What got in the way 1
  • The CEO wanted the full product video on the site → I argued for a ~7-second animation of the product's mission, with the video one click away: in a meeting you present with visual support, you don't narrate over a video, and investors had likely seen the only public video already
  • No investors to test with → tested with the 3 people who'd present it, 4 tasks, counterbalanced order
Outcome 0.5

Used as the live product presentation in investor meetings. 3 of 3 completed all 4 tasks; ~35s → ~8s to find a section. We weren't looking for a designer. Liam reached us and thanks to his work, we closed our first funding round. Yoav Rotem, CTO
