Problem

A stranger, asked for their invoices. Nordic Logic recovers what parcel carriers overcharge. To do it, a one-person company asks a sceptical finance or operations lead for their carrier invoices, then a power of attorney, with no clients, reviews or results to show yet.

The brief I started from, on 28 August, led with price: Nordic Logic’s 35% set against “a 40–50% market standard”.

LateShipment, the competitor a buyer would check first, publishes 35% on its own pricing page. A claim a prospect can disprove in ten minutes turns every careful line around it into marketing.

06 · Challenges — shared; three, then the Claude Code exchanges~1.4 screens · 1440 × ~1260

Challenges

01

The founder's read on the competition didn't hold

Their pricing pageTheir claims pages9 G2 reviews
What happened

Emil saw LateShipment as no real competitor: they didn't file claims themselves, offered no real customer service, and were cheaper because of it.

What I did

I checked each point against their own pages and the nine G2 reviews I'd collected. All three were wrong. When he then wanted the site to compare only against firms that charge more, I pushed back: choosing who you compare against is itself a claim.

What it changed

The site names no rival and makes no comparison it can't defend. The fee is stated as plain fact.

02

No number without a source

What happened

The savings calculator needed a recovery rate, and Nordic Logic had no completed recovery to take one from. An early build showed 2.13%, a number the AI had made up.

What I did

I asked where every figure came from. Published rates run from 1% to 20% and rise with the vendor's cut, so I used the lowest range as a range, then narrowed it to Emil's own estimate.

What it changed

A conservative 1–3% range, stated as an estimate, and the worked examples marked as sample data.

03

Planned what happens after “Send”

Upload→Emil alerted→Sender confirmed→Deleted at 100 days
What happened

Companies upload their carrier invoices to a one-person company. Where do the files go, who sees them, and for how long?

What I did

I mapped every service that touches visitor data and chose analytics without cookies, so the site needs no consent banner. Each upload confirms to the sender and alerts Emil, and the form says the audit works without names, addresses or contents.

What it changed

No cookie banner, and every file is deleted 100 days after upload.

With Claude Code

Claude built the site and drafted the analyses. Every call was mine, and we checked each other.

I caught it
Me

Why 2.12%? Where did you get this number from?

Claude

I made it up, and I was wrong to.

Reverted, and the whole field checked for a real source.

It pushed back
Me

Look at their success stories and make the average of the whole to set a percentage for our calculator.

Claude

An average of vendor claims from 1% to 20% means nothing, so use the lowest, least biased range.

I agreed: a range, never a point figure.
