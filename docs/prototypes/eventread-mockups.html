The problem

Two bands, one lost night

My band's release show lost its audience to a Måneskin arena show on the same night. A friend's band lost theirs to the Champions League final. Nobody had checked, because there was nothing to check with.

Eventread is built for the two people I worked with for ten years: artists and managers picking a date, and promoters and agents filling a room. Both pick a date and hope: ticketing platforms show their own events, and nobody shows the whole night.

Live Nation alone puts on 54,000 shows a year, about 150 every night. Every independent show gets booked around that calendar, usually without seeing it. Prism.fm, TourSmart and ChatGPT each answer part of the question; none checks a date against everything happening that night.

The sources

One search,
two APIs

Ticketmaster covers the big ticketed shows. JamBase catches the smaller, local ones Ticketmaster doesn't list. For Berlin in November 2026 that's 487 shows against Ticketmaster's 93, and 436 of them aren't on Ticketmaster at all. One search checks both, and followed dates send an update when something new lands on your night.

Under the hood

"Cambridge" returned 124 events across the UK and the US until places carried their country (21 after the fix), and tour shows wanting the same night are assigned in two passes.

06 · What got in the way — shared1 screen · 1440 × ~1040
900
Challenges

What got in the way

01

A blank calendar looked safe

Not checkedNo coverage hereAll Saints' Day
What happened

A month with no data looked exactly like a month with no competition. London showed 7 days of events, Tel Aviv came back as a green “No conflicts”, and holidays were missing.

What I did

I gave missing data its own states (“Not checked”, “No coverage here”), showed holidays without scoring them, and added an “announced” indicator built on an 8-city count: about 95% of a month's shows are listed one month out, 1% a year out.

What it changed

London went from 7 visible days to all 31.

02

The score claimed precision it didn't have

SafeThreatCritical
What happened

“Safe 18” on a tour date read like a date or a time, and a 0–100 number promised more precision than the formula has.

What I did

I dropped the number and kept three bands, Safe, Threat and Critical, after checking the cut-points on 3,172 real listings.

What it changed

The formula produced only 27 distinct values, and none fell between 35–45 or 64–80: the bands sit on real gaps.

03

A feature I cut the same day

What happened

Showing the distance to each competing show meant asking for the venue first.

What I did

I removed it. People come to Eventread to find the venue; asking for it first asks them for the answer.

What it changed

People get their answer first; no venue needed to start.

How it stacks at 390

  1. Shell stays: back button left, the three pills right, both 44px tall.
  2. Hero keeps its order: label, title (48px), line, Visit site. The calendar is cropped to the month grid instead of shrinking the whole app to an unreadable thumbnail.
  3. The navy band runs edge to edge. “Your show” sits above the rows; each row keeps its three factors side by side and moves the formula under them, so the band chip stays next to the event name.
  4. No horizontal scrolling anywhere: genre chips wrap, the formula row is full width.
